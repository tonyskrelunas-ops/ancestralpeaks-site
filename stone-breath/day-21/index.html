    Today's challenge · Tool 18 of 22

    The Mountain Within

    Touch your stone this morning, close your eyes, three breaths, and see your place. Then go.

    The tool, as the book gives it

    For anyone who must leave a mountain, a mesa, a homeland, and carry it with them.

    The Gathering – Find a small stone from that place. Not a piece you break off – one that has already separated naturally, already waiting for you. This stone will become your anchor.

    The Offering – Leave something in return. A few drops of water. A breath of gratitude. In the old way, you do not take without giving.

    The Promise – Speak aloud or silently: “I carry you with me. Not as weight – as witness. Wherever I go, you go. I will return when I can. Until then, you are in this stone, and I am in you.”

    Daily Practice (2 minutes) – Each morning, touch your stone. Close your eyes. Breathe three times. See the place in your mind. Let it hold you. Then go.

    For the Reader: Tonas carried a stone from the mesa. He did not use it to pretend he was somewhere else. He used it to remember who he was. You are not leaving your sacred ground. You are learning to carry it.

    Study · Day 21

    What the research found, and what it did not

    He crosses the Alps carrying the mesa. Researchers have a plain name for the bond between a person and a place, and a newer one for the ache when home itself changes. Here is what has been written, with the papers so you can read them yourself.

    What a bond with a place is made of

    This paper gathered the many definitions of place attachment into one frame with three sides: the person (who is attached, alone or as a people), the process (how the bond lives in feeling, thought and action), and the place itself.

    What it did not show: it is a frame for thinking, built from earlier writing. It tests no practice and measures no benefit.

    Scannell, L., & Gifford, R. (2010). Defining place attachment: A tripartite organizing framework. Journal of Environmental Psychology, 30(1), 1–10. doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2009.09.006

    What leaving does

    A psychiatrist reviewed the work of geographers, psychologists and anthropologists and named three ties between a person and their ground: attachment, a bond of care that runs both ways between a person and a loved place; familiarity; and identity, the sense of self drawn from the places of a life. Displacement threatens all three, and nostalgia, disorientation and alienation may follow.

    What it did not show: it is a review that argues a case. It does not measure how many people are affected, or what helps.

    Fullilove, M. T. (1996). Psychiatric implications of displacement: Contributions from the psychology of place. American Journal of Psychiatry, 153(12), 1516–1523. doi.org/10.1176/ajp.153.12.1516

    Homesick without leaving

    This paper gave a name, solastalgia, to the distress people feel when the home they still live in is changed around them. In long drought and beside large open-cut coal mines in New South Wales, people described that distress, made worse by a sense of having no power over the change.

    What it did not show: it names an experience from interviews and surveys in two places. It is not a diagnosis, and no remedy was tested.

    Albrecht, G., Sartore, G.-M., Connor, L., Higginbotham, N., Freeman, S., Kelly, B., Stain, H., Tonna, A., & Pollard, G. (2007). Solastalgia: The distress caused by environmental change. Australasian Psychiatry, 15(Suppl. 1), S95–S98. doi.org/10.1080/10398560701701288

    Fifteen years of that word

    A scoping review mapped fifteen years of writing on solastalgia. Among its four questions, it asked whether that writing engages with Indigenous worldviews and experiences. It calls for work with more kinds of people and places, done together with the people affected, and for clearer ideas.

    What it did not show: there is still no settled way to measure it, and the review tests no practice.

    Galway, L. P., Beery, T., Jones-Casey, K., & Tasala, K. (2019). Mapping the solastalgia literature: A scoping review study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 16(15), 2662. doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16152662

    Where the Mountain Within sits in this

    The research supports the smaller thing: the bond between a person and a place is real, it is part of who a person is, and leaving a place or watching it change is a real loss.

    No study here tested the Mountain Within. That the stone “carries the presence of the place,” and that daily touch “creates neuroplastic reinforcement,” are the book’s words for what the practice does, not findings. The stone, the offering, the promise and the daily touch are the book’s teaching, drawn from people who had to leave their ground and found a way to carry it.

    This is a practice, not medical care. If leaving a home has left a grief that does not ease, tell someone you trust.

    Three questions to think on or write about

    1. What place made you? If you had to carry it in one small thing, what would the thing be?
    2. Austėja lists the things with “no calories and no function” that “are the entire point.” What is on your list?
    3. “Fear is a messenger. You do not kill the messenger.” What has a fear told you mattered, this year?
